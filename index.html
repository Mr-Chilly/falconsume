Bloodline. Diet. Altitude.

As with the world’s most celebrated beef, a great egg is the sum of its lineage, its feed and the life its mother has led. Ours are wild-born, free-flying and conditioned at altitude, producing an egg of remarkable depth, richness and poise.

The peregrine falcon

The fastest animal on earth, and since at least 2011 a resident of the East Mill.

A bird of extraordinary athleticism and lineage, the peregrine has been prized for millennia, from the falconries of medieval courts to the cliffs of the British coast. In its hunting dive it can exceed two hundred miles an hour.

A breeding pair has made its home above the River Derwent for more than a decade. Every egg we serve is gathered on the morning of service and travels fewer than thirty feet from where it was laid to where it is folded: provenance in its purest form.

Terroir aérien

You are what your falcon eats

The character of an egg is the character of everything its mother has eaten. Ours forage freely over the River Gardens, the Derwent and the green spaces of the town.

Their diet is wild, varied and entirely self-selected: the game birds of the riverbank, enriched by the town itself, from the morning’s freshly roasted coffee to the sweet, buttery treats of the playground. It is a diet no farm could replicate, and it gives the yolk its remarkable depth: notes of roast and caramel, a gentle salinity and a long, rounded finish.

Chef de Chivers calls this le goût de la ville: the taste of the town itself.

L’œuf

The egg

A yolk of deep marigold. A white of rare clarity. A shell speckled, from cream to brick red, as though it had been dusted with paprika by somebody who loved it.

Like any great ingredient, it owes everything to breeding and to feed: generations of wild, athletic birds, hunting at altitude on a varied and entirely self-selected diet. The result is an egg of extraordinary richness and depth, with a clean, almost mineral finish that our guests describe, without exception, as unforgettable.
